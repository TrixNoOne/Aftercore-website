process // brief to bench

brief to
bench

A short loop, run often. Each pass produces a physical part and a measured answer — then the numbers decide what gets redrawn.

five passes per revision
revision n+1 · fed by the last measurement

how it gets built

five passes

Nothing moves to the next stage until the previous one returned a part you could hold and a number you could argue with.

01

brief

Weight budget, target feel and the non-negotiables are written down before anything is drawn.

02

cad

Shell and structure modelled with tolerance stacks and wall-thickness limits attached.

03

pcb

Board outline, mounting, sensor placement and routing resolved against the same mechanical model.

04

prototype

Machined or printed parts, real boards, assembled by the people who designed them.

05

validate

Weight, stiffness, actuation and drop data recorded. Findings go straight back into revision n+1.

what each pass returns

numbers, not
adjectives

Four measurements decide whether a revision survives. Recorded on the bench, same fixture, same routine, every single time.

01 / weight

on the scale

Every assembly weighed before and after each change — grams are the budget, not a slogan.

02 / stiffness

under load

Deflection recorded at the shell's worst spots, so a lighter part never becomes a flexible one.

03 / actuation

on the curve

Force and travel checked against the target feel — the switch and the shell tuned as one system.

04 / drop

to the floor

Repeated drops onto the bench until something gives, then the failure is designed out.
